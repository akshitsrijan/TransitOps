## 3. Fleet Operations Manual FAQs

This section provides answers to frequently asked questions regarding vehicle allocation, dispatch, trip planning, and general fleet operational procedures.

### 3.1. Vehicle Allocation and Dispatch

**Q51: How are vehicles allocated for trips?**
A51: Vehicles are allocated based on cargo type, volume, destination, urgency, and vehicle availability, aiming to optimize fleet utilization and meet client requirements.

**Q52: What is the process for dispatching a vehicle?**
A52: The dispatch process involves trip creation in the FMS, driver assignment, document preparation, pre-trip briefing, departure with real-time tracking, in-transit monitoring, arrival/delivery, and post-trip procedures.

**Q53: Can a driver request a specific vehicle?**
A53: While drivers can express preferences, vehicle assignment is primarily based on operational needs, vehicle suitability for the cargo, and availability. Final decision rests with the dispatch team.

**Q54: What happens if a vehicle is not available for a scheduled trip?**
A54: The dispatch team will identify an alternative suitable vehicle from the available fleet. If no internal vehicle is available, approved third-party logistics partners may be engaged.

**Q55: How are urgent or emergency dispatches handled?**
A55: Urgent dispatches are prioritized. The emergency dispatch procedure is activated, involving rapid assessment, resource deployment, and continuous communication until the situation is resolved.

### 3.2. Trip and Route Planning

**Q56: How is a trip planned to ensure efficiency?**
A56: Trip planning focuses on route optimization using specialized software, adherence to realistic schedules, optimal load planning, contingency planning, and regulatory compliance.

**Q57: What factors are considered during route planning?**
A57: Route planning considers road conditions, traffic patterns, tolls, regulatory restrictions, and utilizes GPS-enabled systems for real-time updates and optimal path selection.

**Q58: Can drivers deviate from the planned route?**
A58: Drivers must adhere to planned routes. Deviations are only permitted in emergencies or when instructed by dispatch due to unforeseen circumstances (e.g., road closures, accidents). All deviations must be reported immediately.

**Q59: What is the policy for night driving?**
A59: Night driving requires adherence to specific safety protocols, including specialized training, enhanced vehicle lighting, strict driving hours compliance, mandatory rest periods, and regular check-ins with dispatch.

**Q60: How are interstate transports managed?**
A60: Interstate transports require strict adherence to national and state-specific regulations, including special permits, weight limits, and driving hours. Drivers must carry all required documentation.

### 3.3. Cargo Handling

**Q61: What are the general principles for cargo handling?**
A61: All cargo must be handled with care to prevent damage, loss, or injury. Proper loading, securing, and unloading techniques are paramount, and personnel receive regular training.

**Q62: How should special cargo be handled?**
A62: Special cargo (e.g., high-value, temperature-sensitive) requires specific packaging, dedicated vehicles with specialized features, enhanced security measures, and handling by trained personnel.

**Q63: What are the rules for transporting hazardous goods?**
A63: Transport of hazardous goods is subject to stringent regulations, requiring proper labeling, documentation, driver certification, and detailed emergency response plans.

**Q64: What procedures are in place for oversized cargo?**
A64: Oversized cargo requires meticulous planning, including route surveys, special permits, and, in some cases, escort vehicles, to ensure safe and compliant transport.

**Q65: How is vehicle loading performed safely?**
A65: Safe vehicle loading involves pre-loading inspection, even weight distribution, securing cargo with appropriate devices, and verifying the loaded cargo against the manifest.

**Q66: What is the procedure for vehicle unloading?**
A66: Safe unloading requires site assessment, secure vehicle positioning, cargo verification against the delivery manifest, careful removal using appropriate equipment, and post-unloading inspection.

### 3.4. Vehicle Management and Breakdowns

**Q67: What is the company policy on vehicle idling?**
A67: TransitOps Logistics is committed to minimizing vehicle idling to reduce fuel consumption and emissions. Drivers are instructed to turn off engines when stationary for extended periods, and compliance is monitored.

**Q68: What should a driver do in case of a vehicle breakdown?**
A68: In a breakdown, the driver must prioritize safety, move the vehicle to a safe location if possible, activate hazard lights, place warning triangles, immediately notify emergency dispatch, and remain safe until assistance arrives.

**Q69: When is a replacement vehicle provided?**
A69: A replacement vehicle is dispatched when a breakdown renders the original vehicle inoperable for an extended period, or when repairs cannot be completed on-site, impacting delivery schedules.

**Q70: What is the procedure for getting a replacement vehicle?**
A70: The operations manager authorizes deployment, a suitable replacement vehicle and driver are dispatched, cargo is safely transferred if necessary, and the replacement vehicle continues the trip.

## 4. Driver Handbook FAQs

This section addresses common questions related to driver responsibilities, safe driving practices, and daily operational procedures.

### 4.1. Daily Operations and Inspections

**Q71: What is the purpose of the daily checklist?**
A71: The daily checklist ensures that the vehicle is in optimal condition for operation before commencing any trip, covering critical safety and operational components.

**Q72: What is a Pre-Trip Inspection (PTI) and why is it important?**
A72: A PTI is a thorough inspection performed before each journey to identify potential mechanical issues that could compromise safety or lead to breakdowns. Any defects must be reported immediately.

**Q73: What is a Post-Trip Inspection (PoTI)?**
A73: A PoTI is conducted after completing a trip to identify any damage or issues that may have occurred during the journey, ensuring timely repairs and readiness for the next assignment.

A74: The daily checklist covers tires, brakes, lights, fluids, wipers, mirrors, horn, and safety equipment, ensuring all are functional and in good condition.

## 5. Maintenance Manual FAQs

This section addresses common questions related to vehicle maintenance, repair procedures, and maintenance schedules.

### 5.1. Types of Maintenance

**Q101: What is Preventive Maintenance (PM)?**
A101: Preventive Maintenance (PM) is a proactive approach involving scheduled inspections, servicing, and replacement of parts to prevent breakdowns, extend vehicle life, and ensure continuous operation.

**Q102: What is Corrective Maintenance (CM)?**
A102: Corrective Maintenance (CM) is performed when a vehicle component fails or is found defective during inspections, aiming to restore the vehicle to operational state quickly and efficiently.

**Q103: Why is PM important?**
A103: PM is crucial for maximizing safety, reliability, and longevity of vehicles, minimizing downtime, reducing operational costs, and ensuring compliance with safety regulations.

**Q104: How often are maintenance intervals determined?**
A104: Maintenance intervals are determined based on vehicle type, mileage, operating conditions, and manufacturer recommendations. A detailed schedule is maintained in the Fleet Management System (FMS).

**Q105: What is the company's policy on parts replacement?**
A105: Only genuine or approved OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) equivalent parts are to be used for all repairs and replacements to ensure compatibility, reliability, and maintain vehicle warranty.

### 5.2. Key Maintenance Procedures

**Q106: How often should engine oil and filters be changed?**
A106: Engine oil and filter changes are performed at regular intervals as specified by the vehicle manufacturer or every 10,000 km, whichever comes first.

**Q107: How often are brake systems inspected?**
A107: Brake systems are inspected every 20,000 km or as part of every second oil change. Worn components are replaced immediately.

**Q108: When are tires replaced or rotated?**
A108: Tires are inspected daily. Rotation is performed every 20,000 km for even wear. Replacement occurs when tread depth falls below legal limits or significant damage is observed.

**Q109: How is battery maintenance performed?**
A109: Batteries are inspected for corrosion, terminal tightness, and charge level during routine service. Water levels are topped up, and batteries are replaced every 2-3 years or when they fail to hold a charge.

**Q110: How often are air filters replaced?**
A110: Engine air filters are replaced every 40,000 km or more frequently in dusty environments, as indicated by inspection.

**Q111: What is the schedule for coolant replacement?**
A111: Engine coolant is checked during every service and replaced every 80,000 km or every 2 years, whichever comes first.

**Q112: How are engine diagnostics performed?**
A112: Advanced diagnostic tools are used for troubleshooting engine issues. Regular diagnostic checks are part of PM and are performed immediately if a fault indicator light illuminates.

**Q113: What is the transmission service schedule?**
A113: Transmission fluid and filter are changed according to manufacturer specifications, typically every 100,000 km.

**Q114: How is the suspension system maintained?**
A114: Suspension components are inspected during every major service for wear and damage, crucial for vehicle stability and driver comfort.

**Q115: What does electrical system maintenance involve?**
A115: The entire electrical system, including wiring, lights, and charging system, is inspected during routine maintenance. Faulty components are repaired or replaced.

**Q116: How is the air conditioning system maintained?**
A116: AC systems are checked annually for refrigerant levels, leaks, and proper functioning. Filters are replaced as needed.

**Q117: What is involved in emission system maintenance?**
A117: Emission control systems are inspected and maintained to ensure compliance with environmental regulations, including DPF, SCR, and EGR systems.

**Q118: How is the fuel system maintained?**
A118: Fuel filters are replaced at regular intervals (typically every 40,000 km), and fuel lines and tanks are inspected for leaks and damage.

**Q119: What is the lubrication schedule for vehicles?**
A119: All critical moving parts are lubricated according to the vehicle manufacturer’s recommendations, typically during every service interval.

### 5.3. Workshop Procedures

**Q120: What are the safety protocols in the workshop?**
A120: All workshop activities adhere to strict safety protocols, including the use of PPE, proper handling of tools and chemicals, and maintaining a clean and organized workspace.

**Q121: What is the repair workflow in the workshop?**
A121: The workflow includes vehicle reception, diagnosis, repair authorization (for non-routine repairs), repair execution, quality control, and vehicle handover with updated maintenance records.

**Q122: How is quality control ensured after repairs?**
A122: After repairs, a quality control check and road test are performed to ensure the issue is resolved and the vehicle is safe for operation.

**Q123: What is the purpose of the inspection checklist?**
A123: A comprehensive inspection checklist is used for all preventive maintenance services to ensure all critical components are thoroughly examined and serviced.

**Q124: Who authorizes non-routine repairs?**
A124: For non-routine repairs, authorization is obtained from the Fleet Manager before execution.

**Q129: What PPE is generally required for employees?**
A129: General PPE includes high-visibility safety vests when near traffic, steel-toed safety shoes in workshops, and appropriate gloves for handling cargo or maintenance.

### 6.2. Fire Safety

**Q130: What fire prevention measures are in place?**
A130: Measures include a no-smoking policy, proper storage of flammable materials, electrical safety checks, and regular inspections for fire hazards.

**Q131: Are fire extinguishers available in vehicles?**
A131: Yes, all company vehicles and premises are equipped with appropriate fire extinguishers, and employees are trained in their proper use.

**Q132: What is the procedure in case of a vehicle fire?**
A132: In a vehicle fire, stop safely, turn off the engine, evacuate all occupants to a safe distance, call emergency services (101), and notify dispatch. Only attempt to extinguish if safe.

**Q133: What should be done in case of an electrical fire?**
A133: If safe, switch off the engine and disconnect the battery. Use a Class C (electrical) fire extinguisher. Do not use water. Evacuate and call emergency services if the fire spreads.

### 6.3. Medical and Hazardous Emergencies

**Q134: What should be done in a medical emergency?**
A134: For serious injuries, immediately call an ambulance (102). Provide first aid if trained and safe. Notify TransitOps Logistics dispatch immediately.

**Q135: What is the procedure for a hazardous material spill?**
A135: Prioritize personal safety, attempt to contain the spill if safe, immediately call emergency services (HAZMAT team), and notify TransitOps Logistics dispatch.

**Q136: What first aid equipment is available?**
A136: All company vehicles are equipped with a first-aid kit, and key personnel receive basic first-aid training.

**Q137: What should I do if exposed to chemicals?**
A137: Identify the chemical if possible, follow first aid guidelines (e.g., flush skin/eyes with water), seek immediate medical attention, and notify dispatch.

### 6.4. Natural Disasters

**Q138: What is the procedure during an earthquake while driving?**
A138: Pull over to a clear area away from buildings, bridges, and overhead wires. Stay inside the vehicle. After, check for injuries and follow emergency service instructions.

**Q139: What is the policy regarding driving through flooded areas?**
A139: Never attempt to drive through flooded roads. Turn around, don't drown. Report flooded routes to dispatch.

**Q140: What are the warning signs of a landslide?**
A140: Warning signs include cracks in roads, leaning trees, or unusual sounds. If observed, move away from the path to a safe area and notify authorities.
